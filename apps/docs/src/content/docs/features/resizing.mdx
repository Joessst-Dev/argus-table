Drag the handle on a header's end edge, or focus a header and press Alt+Arrow to resize it by 10 px; a double-click on the handle fits the column to its content <Since since="0.2" />. Widths stay within `minWidth` and `maxWidth`, a resized flex column becomes fixed, and a column with `resizable: false` has no handle.

## Saving widths

`columnSizing` holds the resized widths by column id. Set it and the table is controlled: each resize proposes new widths in `argus-column-sizing-change`, and your code applies them, here saving them to `localStorage` too, so they survive a reload. Read saved state once the page runs in the browser, not while rendering on a server. To save once a drag ends rather than on every frame, listen to `argus-column-resize-end` <Since since="1.1" />.

## Columns that fill the width

With `stretchColumns` (the attribute `stretch-columns`), the columns fill the table's width: when they're narrower than the table, they stretch in proportion to their widths, up to their `maxWidth`. When the table gets narrower, they shrink back to their own widths, never below, and past that the table scrolls sideways as usual.

A column you resize keeps the width you give it, and the other columns share the space it frees; once you've resized every column, the last one takes the free space. Pinned columns and the checkbox and chevron columns keep their widths. A `{ flex }` column already takes the free space, so nothing stretches while one is shown. `columnSizing`, `getState()` and the resize events hold each column's own width, never a stretched one, so a saved layout fits any width. Here the # column stays at its `maxWidth`.
