`columnGroups` puts headers over several columns: `{ id, header, columns }`, where `columns` lists column ids. `columns` itself stays a flat list, and so do sorting, widths, order and events. Columns without a group sit under an empty filler, and the group headers take the header's tokens and the parts `header-group-row`, `header-group-cell` and `header-group-filler`.

A group's columns stay together: drag a group header to move the whole group, or press Alt+Shift+Arrow on it; a column moves only within its group, and `argus-column-order-change` carries the `groupId`. Group headers are part of the keyboard navigation: ArrowUp from a column header goes to its group, ArrowDown back to the column, and Left and Right move between the groups of a row. Groups don't sort.

## Nested groups

Groups nest, and each level adds a header row. Here "Shipping" sits inside "Who", over City alone: three header rows. Groups are top-aligned, so a column with fewer groups over it has fillers below its groups.

## Groups as children

`<argus-column-group>` (`<ArgusColumnGroup>` in React and Vue) writes the groups as markup, around column children and other groups. A non-empty `columnGroups` property wins over them. A group's header can be content other than text: a function in HTML and React, a `#group-<id>` slot in Vue, an `argusGroupHeader` template in Angular. Give it a name for text mode too (the `header` text, or the function's answer when it's asked for `text`), so announcements and plugins can name the group.

## Pinned columns in a group

A group whose columns are pinned to different sides shows on each side: here Order is pinned to the start, so "Ids" has a sticky part over it and a scrolling part over Reference. Scroll sideways: the pinned part stays.

## Resizing a group

Drag the handle on a group's edge, or press Alt+Arrow on a focused group, to resize it: the change is spread over its resizable columns in proportion to their widths, and `argus-column-sizing-change` fires for each column. A double-click fits them to their content. `reorderable: false` keeps a group in place ("Who" here); its columns still move within it.
