Columns can be children of the table instead of the `columns` property: `<argus-column>` in HTML and Angular, `<ArgusColumn>` in React and Vue. Attributes and props cover the plain fields: `column-id`, `header`, `accessor` (a path), `width` (px, `auto` or a flex share such as `1fr`), `min-width`, `max-width`, `pin`, `align`, and the flags `sortable`, `resizable` and `reorderable`. Content that depends on the row comes from the column's `cell`, a `#cell-<id>` slot in Vue or an `argusCell` template in Angular. In plain HTML, a `<template>` child gives every cell the same content.

A non-empty `columns` property wins over the children.

## Columns that come and go

The table follows its children: add, remove or move a column, or change one of its attributes, and the table updates, keeping widths, order and sorting by `column-id`. So conditionals and loops (`.map()`, `v-for`, `@for`) just work.
